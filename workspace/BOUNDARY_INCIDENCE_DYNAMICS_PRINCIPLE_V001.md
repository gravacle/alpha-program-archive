# Boundary Incidence Dynamics Principle v001

Date: 2026-07-23

## Logical status

This is a target-value-free, target-aware, forward-sealed Level-1 Gravacle
principle. It supplies new microscopic dynamics; it is not presented as a
theorem of the older carrier, co-registration, compactness, or record-onset
results.

The historical program knows the electromagnetic target. Historical target
blindness is therefore not claimed. No measured coupling, particle mass,
cosmological endpoint, fitted coefficient, or previous charged-branch
operator may enter the construction or select among competitors. A genuinely
unused consequence remains mandatory downstream.

## First-opening incidence data

Let `v_0` denote the common pre-record alternative. Let `h_a`, for
`a=1,...,r`, denote the independent primitive public handles.

The first-opening incidence complex is generated by the following rules:

```text
one root v_0;
one primitive outward transition for each independent handle h_a;
one new endpoint alternative reached by that transition;
no extra endpoint without an additional public distinction;
no mixed/composite endpoint before composition generates it;
no duplicate primitive arrow carrying the same handle and orientation.
```

These rules define the smallest connected rooted oriented incidence support
generated by one application of the primitive handles. Whether they force a
unique rooted complex, rather than merely describe one, is a theorem
obligation below.

Every primitive oriented incidence arrow carries exactly the primitive unit
action character. Reversing orientation gives the adjoint character. No
handle-specific positive amplitude is attached to an elementary arrow.

The statement "unit arrow" is the new physical content of BID. It means that
the coefficient of an elementary incidence is fixed by incidence sign and
the unit action character, not by later response matching. A common
dimensionful conversion from the dimensionless incidence operator to the
physical generator must come from the independently derived causal record
interval. BID does not set that interval numerically.

## Candidate operator implied by the principle

For a derived oriented complex `K`, let

```text
C_p(K)
```

be its complex `p`-chain space with the elementary oriented cells as
orthonormal basis vectors. Let `d_chi` be the character-twisted incidence
map. Its nonzero matrix elements are incidence signs multiplied by primitive
unit characters.

The candidate dimensionless Boundary Incidence operator is

```text
B_chi = d_chi + d_chi^dagger
```

on the graded chain carrier. It is self-adjoint and odd under chain parity.
Its square supplies the corresponding incidence Laplacians.

This displayed formula is a candidate consequence, not yet the uniqueness
theorem. The theorem must show that every normalized, self-adjoint, odd,
first-incidence, character-covariant transition operator is related to
`B_chi` only by:

```text
orientation reversal;
cell-basis rephasing or relabeling;
gauge-equivalent character representatives;
and one common dimensionful scale fixed outside the relative-incidence
problem.
```

If a physically inequivalent relative weighting or extra primitive block
survives, operator uniqueness fails.

## Primitive versus effective terms

BID does not assert that Maxwell, Pauli, or other effective operators never
occur. It asserts:

```text
no independent primitive F^2 coefficient;
no independent primitive Pauli/clover coefficient;
no handle-specific primitive transition normalization.
```

Curvature response, Pauli structure, thresholds, and other effective terms
may and generally must be generated by the spectrum, reduction, determinant,
and coarse graining of the one microscopic incidence operator. Their
coefficients must then be computed from that operator rather than attached
independently.

## Required theorem gates

No downstream response or alpha evaluation is authorized until all of these
gates pass.

### 1. Carrier-selection theorem

Starting only from the inherited continuous additive action character and
the first-opening incidence rules, determine whether the physical carrier is
forced. In particular, adjudicate the previously surviving comparison
models:

```text
finite exponent-two sign frame;
finite cyclic quadrature frame;
continuous faithful action-character model.
```

A connected continuous action group cannot be replaced by a nontrivial
finite image without loss of faithful continuous action information. The
full proof and the generated-support consequence must be explicit.

### 2. Operator-uniqueness theorem

Enumerate the complete admissible operator space on the derived carrier and
prove that its physical quotient contains exactly one relative coupling ray.
Writing down `B_chi` is not the proof.

### 3. Relative-rescaling forbidding lemma

Show that rescaling any local, edge, handle, or plaquette sector relative to
the others violates a stated BID property: primitive unit-character
normalization, composition, self-adjoint incidence, covariance, or generated
support. A verbal prohibition is insufficient.

### 4. Primitive-addition forbidding lemma

Enumerate symmetry-compatible primitive competitors, including Pauli/clover
and independent curvature-stiffness additions. Prove that each is either:

```text
generated downstream by B_chi;
gauge/basis equivalent;
or incompatible with the primitive first-incidence operator class.
```

### 5. Frozen-parent reproduction test

Derive the BID operator without consulting the frozen parent, then compare.
Agreement supplies provenance. Disagreement corrects the parent upstream.
No coefficient may be changed to improve agreement.

### 6. New-consequence gate

BID must fix at least one item not supplied as an input, such as:

```text
the comparison-carrier selection;
the generated carrier dimension;
a mixed-sector sign;
a previously free relative weight;
or a previously free incidence constraint.
```

A principle that only restates the old parent does not pass.

## Failure rule

The BID route fails or remains open if:

```text
more than one inequivalent first-opening complex survives;
more than one relative coupling ray survives;
an independent primitive Pauli or F^2 coefficient remains admissible;
the unique operator is selected by comparison with alpha or a mass;
the frozen parent is silently substituted for the derived operator;
or no genuinely new consequence is produced.
```

## Scope

If discharged, BID can close the primitive normalization and operator-choice
gaps and can potentially remove the finite-stiffness ambiguity by replacing
an adjustable continuum action with one complete microscopic operator.

BID does not, merely by being stated, derive:

```text

the physical source mass;
the causal record interval or volume;
the complete durable environment;
the full matter spectrum;
the finite electromagnetic response;
or alpha.
```

Those results require the theorem gates and downstream dynamics.
