# BID First-Opening Graph Refinement Quotient v001

Date: 2026-07-23

## Purpose

Distinguish a harmless subdivision of one first-opening transition from a
physically enlarged record graph.

## Elementary refinement equivalence

Consider a connected oriented source-record graph with:

```text
one unresolved root;
one public first-record endpoint;
no other public vertex labels;
and unitary edge transports.
```

An internal vertex is presentation-only when it is:

```text
unlabeled;
not durable;
of directed indegree one and outdegree one;
and carries no independent source, handle, or environment datum.
```

The elementary reduction

```text
r --e_1--> v --e_2--> p
  -> r --e--> p
```

uses

```text
U_e=U_(e_2) U_(e_1).
```

Its inverse is an invisible bivalent subdivision. Equivalence is generated by
these moves, edge reparametrization, and label-preserving graph isomorphism.

## Reduced representative

Every finite directed linear path satisfying the conditions above reduces to
one root, one endpoint, and one edge. Associativity of transport composition
makes the reduced transport independent of reduction order.

The following do not reduce to that representative:

```text
parallel primitive paths:
  retain a nonzero cycle rank after forgetting orientation;

a branch:
  has a vertex with indegree or outdegree greater than one;

an intermediate durable/public label:
  cannot be removed by the refinement rule;

separate chiral endpoints:
  add public endpoint labels;

a loop or face:
  adds cycle/composition data.
```

Thus an unlabeled bivalent subdivision is the same physical presentation,
while these competitors are enlarged record objects.

## Earned result and boundary

Within the declared one-root/one-public-endpoint acyclic first-opening class,
the one-arrow graph is the unique reduced representative. This is a graph
refinement theorem, not a proof that nature permits only that class. Enlarged
objects remain valid later branches.
