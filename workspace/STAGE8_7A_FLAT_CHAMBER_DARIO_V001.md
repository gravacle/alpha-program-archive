### 2.2 Where the moves act

[PROVABLE] An arrow of `Ref_a` is a map of complexes whose data is a partition of cells and
an incidence relabeling. `IDX` `66f078ba…` §1.3: *"`A1` and `A2` each replace a cell by a

### 2.3 The derivation

[PROVABLE] **Claim.** Every arrow `g` of `Ref_a` — `A0`, `A1`, `A2` and every finite
composite — preserves `(eta, epsilon)`.

*Proof.*

1. **What "touching" would mean.** For `g` to alter `(eta, epsilon)` its action would need a
   component on the ordered-bivector-basis signature — i.e. `g` would have to carry a map on
   the index algebra in which `J_star` is written.
2. **No generator has such a component.** `A1` and `A2` are specified entirely by how they
   partition a cell (§2.2); `A0` by relabeling and ratified restriction. None carries any
   index-algebra factor. The generators' action on `(eta, epsilon)` is therefore the identity
   — not by a compatibility argument, but because the product decomposes and the factor is
   absent.
3. **Composites inherit it.** If each `g_i` acts as the identity on that factor, so does
   `g_n ∘ … ∘ g_1`, a composition of identities on that factor.
4. Hence `(eta, epsilon)` is invariant under every arrow of `Ref_a`. ∎

[YOURS] **The derivation is sound and its own shape is its limit.** This is invariance
**by absence of action**, not invariance **by compatibility**. Nothing was shown to survive
a transformation; it was shown that no transformation is applied. Everything in the archive
preserves `(eta, epsilon)` in exactly this sense, including every object the clause is
supposed to exclude.
